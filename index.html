How to play

Life at cell scale

WASD or arrow keys to swim, or hold the mouse button (or a finger) where you want to go. Shift or Sprint pushes harder for a lot of energy.

Water is thick for something this small: you stop the moment your motor stops, and currents carry you. You start as a bacterium that twitches along on pili. A rotary flagellum runs and tumbles; chemoreceptors steer the tumbles.

Feed and divide

A simple cell absorbs food it touches through its membrane, or makes its own from light or sulfur. Surplus energy fills the Division bar; press R to divide and open the editor.

Spend DNA on parts, and take one free mutation shaped by what this generation went through. The editor shows how your parts work together, and where they get in each other’s way.

Simple cell → complex cell → colony

After oxygen appears, oxygen-breathing bacteria look for a larger cell to live in. Let one slip inside, then divide: keep it as a partner and you become a complex cell with a mitochondrion, a nucleus and new parts (pseudopods, cilia, food vacuoles, eyes).

Complex cells can engulf smaller cells whole. With adhesion proteins, daughters stay attached: a colony of 4 completes the stage.

Dangers

Each spot has a temperature, light, UV, oxygen and sulfur level; the gauges show where you stand. Eras change the whole sea, and nights stop photosynthesis.

Viruses target whatever species is most common; CRISPR genes give lasting immunity. A rotifer may pass by: stay out of its feeding swirl. A cyst (C) lets you sleep through almost anything, but you can’t eat.
